## 👩‍💻 About This Portfolio

This is my Agile portfolio for the second semester of my Diploma in Information and Communication Technology at the Cape Peninsula University of Technology. It shows the Agile work my team and I did on **Kasi-Book**, a simple and affordable booking and order management platform for small township businesses such as barbers, nail technicians, salon owners, kota vendors and mechanics.

**Team members:** Ncebakazi Ndiza (Scrum Master), Somila Ndoboza (Backend and Database Lead) and Rose Molakeng (UX/UI Designer, Frontend Developer and Tester)

🔗 [Kasi-Book Trello Board](https://trello.com/b/4K3Rs1nR)

## 1. Agile Team Charter

### Evidence

📄 [Download our Agile Team Charter (Word document)](Agile_Team_Charter.docx)

Our charter explains why our team exists, who is on the team and what each member does, how we work (Scrum, daily stand-ups, planning, review and retrospective), our working agreements, how we communicate, how we make decisions, our Definition of Done, our core values, and the risks and constraints of the project.

### Reflection

**Situation:**
At the start of the project, our team of three had to agree on how we would work together as an Agile team before building Kasi-Book.

**Task:**
My task was to help create the Agile Team Charter so that everyone understood the purpose of the project, their role, and the rules we would follow.

**Action:**
We discussed the problem we are solving for township entrepreneurs, and we agreed on our roles, ceremonies, communication channels, working agreements and Definition of Done. I was given the role of UX/UI Designer, Frontend Developer and Tester, so I made sure that the parts of the charter about design approval and UI standards were clear. We also listed risks such as inconsistent availability, time management and learning Agile while we work.

**Result:**
We completed a charter that the whole team can refer back to. I learned that a charter is not just a document to submit, because it helps the team stay accountable and gives us something to return to when we are stuck. It also showed me how important clear roles and communication are in a team.

## 2. User Stories and Tasks

### Evidence

Each Trello card has a user story in the description and a checklist of subtasks, and every subtask is given to a team member. Below is the Sprint 1 (Day 1) card, **Project Setup & Design**.

![User story and subtasks on Trello](images/user-stories-and-tasks.png)

| Item | Detail |
|------|--------|
| **User story** | As a dev team, we want the environment and design set up, so we can start building smoothly. |
| **Subtask 1** | Create GitHub repo and branching strategy (Ncebakazi) |
| **Subtask 2** | Set up local/dev environments for all team members (Rose) |
| **Subtask 3** | Agree on coding standards and commit conventions (Somila) |

### Reflection

**Situation:**
Before we could start building, we had to break the project into small pieces of work that each team member could understand and complete.

**Task:**
My task was to help write user stories and divide them into smaller tasks, with a clear owner for each task.

**Action:**
We used the format "As a ..., we want ..., so that ..." for the user stories and added them to the descriptions of our Trello cards. Then we broke each story into subtasks in a checklist and wrote the name of the team member next to each one. For example, on the Project Setup & Design card I was responsible for setting up the local development environments.

**Result:**
Everyone knew what they were responsible for and we could see our progress through the checklist percentage on every card. I learned that writing from the user's point of view keeps the team focused on the value of the work, and that small tasks with names next to them are much easier to complete than a big vague task.

We needed one place that showed everything that must be done to deliver Kasi-Book, from design to testing and deployment.

**Task:**

**Action:**
As a team we listed all the work needed for the project, such as the frontend, backend, database, security, documentation and testing. We put these on our Trello board in the Product Backlog list and used the checklists and member tags to show who is involved. ### Evidence

Our sprint backlog is made up of the cards on the Trello board for each sprint. Every sprint has cards for Day 1 to Day 4, and each card has a checklist and assigned members.

![Sprint Backlog on Trello](images/sprint-backlog.png)

**Sprint 1**

| Day | Task |
|-----|------|

| Day 2 | UI/UX Wireframing |

**Sprint 2**

| Day | Task |
|-----|------|
| Day 1 | Customer Booking & Ordering |
| Day 2 | Date & Time Selection |
| Day 3 | Booking Confirmation |
| Day 4 | Payment screen/confirmation |

**Sprint 3**

| Day | Task |
|-----|------|
| Day 1 | Develop frontend layout based on wireframes |
| Day 2 | Create reusable frontend components |
| Day 3 | Manage application data using state management |

**Sprint 4**

| Day | Task |
|-----|------|
| Day 1 | Improve application performance and loading speed |
| Day 2 | Implement user registration and login functionality |
| Day 3 | Create business profile and settings feature |

### Reflection

**Situation:**
After the product backlog was ready, we had to decide what we would work on in each sprint.

**Task:**
My task was to help plan the sprints and complete my own cards in each sprint.

**Action:**
We took items from the product backlog and moved them into Sprints 1 to 4, with one card for each day. We started with project setup, wireframes and research in Sprint 1, then moved to the booking and ordering screens in Sprint 2, and then to building the frontend layout and components in Sprint 3. In Sprint 4 we worked on performance, login and the business profile. Each card has a checklist, and we ticked the items off as they were completed.

**Result:**
The board shows that the finished cards have all their checklist items completed. I learned that small sprints keep the work manageable, and that the sprint backlog helps the team to focus on only what we committed to. It also taught me to plan my time better, because each card has a clear day and a clear outcome.

## 5. Agile Logbook

### Evidence

📄 [Download my Agile Logbook for Sprint 1 (Word document)](Rose-Agile_LogBook.docx)

My logbook has a taskboard (product backlog, in progress and completed), a walkthrough of the sprint, and a daily stand-up log for each day of Sprint 1, where I answered what I completed, what I plan to do next, and what my impediments were.

### Reflection

**Situation:**
During Sprint 1 we had to keep a daily record of our work so that the team and our lecturer could see our progress.

**Task:**
My task was to fill in my Agile Logbook every day of the sprint, using the daily stand-up questions.

**Action:**
Each day I wrote what I had completed, what I planned to do next and which impediments I had. I also updated the taskboard so that the finished Sprint 1 work moved to Completed, and I answered the walkthrough questions about what the team needs to finish and what is slowing us down. I kept my logbook in line with the Trello board so that they showed the same information.

**Result:**
The logbook gave me a clear record of the whole sprint, and it helped me notice problems such as time management and team availability early. I learned that writing things down every day keeps me accountable and makes it easier to reflect during the retrospective. It is a habit I want to keep using in future projects.

## Overall Reflection

Working on Kasi-Book showed me that Agile is about people and communication as much as it is about tools. The Agile Team Charter helped us agree on how to work, the user stories and tasks made the work clear, the product and sprint backlogs helped us to plan and see our progress, and the logbook helped me to reflect every day. It was not always easy to balance the project with my other modules, but using Trello and the logbook kept me organised. I now feel more confident to work in an Agile team and to apply these skills in my future career.
